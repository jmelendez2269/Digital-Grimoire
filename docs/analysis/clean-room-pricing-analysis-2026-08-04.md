# Prismarium clean-room pricing analysis

Its three-tier packaging recommendation has been superseded by `docs/analysis/prism-credit-four-tier-model-2026-08-05.md` following the decision to evaluate a four-tier Prism Credit system.

**Prepared:** August 4, 2026; updated August 5, 2026  
**Scope:** Active product code, live production behavior and data, and current official competitor pages only.  
**Clean-room rule:** Existing Prismarium pricing proposals, pricing audits, and historical business plans were deliberately excluded.

## Executive decision

Prismarium is underpriced **as a complete learning-and-research system**, but it is not yet packaged consistently enough to support an immediate across-the-board increase.

The product already contains more substantive value than many high-priced self-study offers: an original multi-course curriculum, nearly complete reading digests, a primary-source library, a large sourced knowledge graph, a genuinely differentiated Seven Lenses research engine, Deep Search, a connected journal/workbook, and an emerging graph-grounded practice tool. Its strongest value is the integrated journey:

> guided question → source reading → multi-perspective research → connected notes → original synthesis → applied practice

The present commercial problem is not a lack of value. It is that the application currently gives almost every paid feature to every paid tier, exposes several costly AI tools without reliable metering, promises limits that differ from enforcement, and has not yet turned course completion into a reliable outcome. Prismarium is not ready to sell Adept as a distinct outcome, so the active offer should be simplified to Reader, Student and Scholar while the Adept name is reserved for the future.

Prismarium will also sit beside a creator-led YouTube channel with substantial free public education. That strengthens the model: YouTube is an open broadcast and discovery layer, while Prismarium is the owned membership, learning and research layer. Members should primarily pay for structure, tools, saved work, deeper participation and meaningful outcomes; occasional supplementary creator material may also be reserved for members without becoming the core value promise.

### Recommended price path

| Tier, least to most value | Current public price | Price while product is being aligned | Launch-ready target | Annual target | Primary outcome |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|
| **The Reader** | $0 | **$0** | **$0** | **$0** | Explore trustworthy sources and begin a personal knowledge practice |
| **The Student** | $15/month | **$15/month, labeled Founding** | **$19/month** | **$190/year** | Complete guided study paths and build course workbooks |
| **The Scholar** | $29/month | **$29/month, labeled Founding** | **$39/month** | **$390/year** | Use Prismarium as a standalone independent-research workspace |
| **The Adept** | $49/month | **Remove from new sales** | **Not priced yet** | **Not offered** | Reserve the name until an applied-practice outcome genuinely exists |

Student and Scholar annual prices equal ten months of monthly billing, a transparent 16.7% discount. Scholar should be the recommended plan.

Do **not** activate the target prices merely by changing the pricing page. Activate them for new customers after the launch gates in this report are met. Grandfather continuously subscribed founding members at their current price for at least 12 months; permanent grandfathering is an even stronger trust signal if the subscriber base is still small.

The targets are intentionally far below a $997 headline offer. Prismarium is a recurring membership, not a one-time certificate course. A future Adept plan should not receive a price until it has a human-supported or genuinely applied outcome such as reviewed capstone work, live feedback or a verifiable portfolio. A downloadable e-certificate by itself does not justify a premium tier.

## YouTube and the direct-membership model

> **Internal strategy:** Prismarium can perform the economic job of a Patreon-style membership without describing itself that way to customers or discussing platform economics.

The customer-facing distinction should be simple:

- **YouTube teaches and invites:** free videos, public ideas, demonstrations, essays and conversations.
- **Reader helps people explore:** sources, graph connections, a free orientation and a personal notebook.
- **Student helps people finish:** sequenced courses, reading routes, workbooks, artifacts and capstones.
- **Scholar helps people investigate:** Seven Lenses, Deep Search, research history and connected evidence.
- **Adept is reserved for later:** it should return only when Prismarium can offer a distinct applied-practice outcome.

This makes “free” part of the brand rather than a temporary acquisition trick. A viewer should never feel that a YouTube episode is an incomplete advertisement whose missing conclusion is behind a paywall. The paid product should make the free knowledge more actionable, organized, persistent and personal.

### Recommended public language

Appropriate:

- “Free public videos and resources will remain part of Prismarium.”
- “Prismarium is where you can follow the sources, take a guided path, save your work and investigate further.”
- “Paid memberships support new public work while giving members a deeper learning and research workspace.”

Avoid:

- Describing Prismarium publicly as a Patreon replacement.
- Explaining YouTube revenue share, fees or internal cost avoidance.
- Implying that payment buys personal access to the founder unless a specific, sustainable live benefit is promised.
- Locking cited sources, corrections or essential context behind payment merely to force conversion.

### Product and funnel implications

1. Link each relevant video to a free companion page containing its sources, related Library entries and graph concepts.
2. Let viewers create a Reader account to save, annotate and continue exploring without entering payment details.
3. Map video themes to PRE and public course previews; let structured completion be the Student upgrade.
4. Make the in-app YouTube library public or Reader-accessible once it is populated.
5. Use one primary invitation per video: “Continue the investigation in Prismarium,” not a list of subscription features.
6. Let Reader members make an optional one-time contribution or sponsor a Community Access seat without adding a confusing fifth tier.
7. Treat creator support as a secondary emotional reason to join. Every paid tier must still deliver enough functional value to stand on its own.

This channel strategy improves trust and lowers dependence on paid acquisition, but it does not by itself justify a higher price. The active $19/$39 targets remain grounded in the member outcomes delivered inside Prismarium.

## Two paid entry paths, not one forced course ladder

Prismarium has at least two equally legitimate paid personas:

1. **Guided learner:** wants a path, sequence, assignments and completion. This person chooses Student.
2. **Independent researcher/tool user:** wants the Library, Knowledge Graph, Seven Lenses, Deep Search, journal and The Working, with no intention of enrolling in a course. This person chooses Scholar.

The pricing page should therefore say **“Choose how you want to use Prismarium”** above Student and Scholar. They can remain ordered by price and entitlements, but they should not be presented as Beginner Course → Advanced Course. Scholar is not “more school”; it is the tool-first route.

### Recommended structure

| Tier | Main persona | What the member is buying | Course participation required? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Reader | Curious explorer | A free research foundation | No |
| Student | Guided learner | Structure, progression, workbooks and completion | Courses are the point |
| Scholar | Independent researcher | A serious standalone research and knowledge workspace | **No** |
| Adept | Future practitioner persona | Not for sale until a distinct applied-practice outcome exists | No active plan |

Do not add another “Tools” tier. Scholar already has that natural role. The active three-card offer should be Reader, Student and Scholar; Adept should be hidden from checkout or shown only as a future waitlist. Likewise, do not remove course access from Scholar merely to make the package look pure: released courses have very low marginal delivery cost, may become useful later, and can be included without being the reason a researcher pays.

The right distinction is in the promise and default experience:

- Student sees current course, next reading, workbook and completion progress first.
- Scholar sees recent investigations, saved searches, graph trails, journal connections and remaining fair-use allowances first.

Ask this during onboarding as a preference—not as another entitlement decision:

> What brings you to Prismarium today?  
> Follow a guided path / Research independently

The selection should personalize navigation and dashboard emphasis. It should never permanently lock the member out of another part of the product.

### What “course-aware access” means

This is the implementation that makes “course-linked tool exercises included” truthful. It does **not** exist today. Current course links only open `/seven-lenses` or `/search` with a prefilled prompt. The receiving APIs see the member and subscription tier, but not a verified course, week or exercise, so they cannot distinguish coursework from independent research.

A production-ready flow should work like this:

1. **Give every product-practice exercise a stable ID.** Store its course, week, tool, prompt, required/optional status and included attempts. Current format-v2 practice is embedded in course content and needs a stable entitlement key.
2. **Launch by assignment identity, not a trusted prompt parameter.** A link may look like `/seven-lenses?assignment=...`; the server retrieves the authoritative prompt and tool from course data.
3. **Verify eligibility on the server.** Confirm that the member is authenticated, enrolled, entitled to the release-open course, and using the tool named by that assignment.
4. **Use a separate course ledger.** Record `user`, `assignment`, `tool`, `status`, `successful_runs`, `intentional_regenerations`, timestamps and generation ID in a `course_tool_runs`-style table.
5. **Route usage to the correct bucket.** An eligible assignment-scoped run uses the course entitlement. A tool opened independently uses the member's Open Inquiry or Scholar fair-use policy.
6. **Count only successful generations.** Technical failures do not consume an attempt. Include one intentional regeneration per assignment so an unusable but technically successful response cannot block learning.
7. **Return the work to the course.** Offer “Save to this week's workbook” and associate the output with the correct course/week artifact.
8. **Explain it in the interface.** Display “Included with C01, Week 3—this will not use your monthly Open Inquiry” before the run.
9. **Prevent easy quota laundering.** Do not trust course IDs, prompts or tool names from the browser alone. Validate against server-side curriculum data and rate-limit automated/repeated calls.

Current evidence:

- `CourseLearnerRenderer.tsx` builds tool links from the title and prompt and sometimes passes a course graph view, but no entitlement identity.
- The legacy learner links directly to Seven Lenses with only a prompt and lens settings.
- `checkRateLimit(userId)` currently receives only the user ID and applies a tier-wide limit.

This is a bounded feature rather than a vague pricing idea. It requires course-content identifiers, a usage table, API authorization changes, rate-limit routing and small course/tool UI changes.

## What the product actually contains today

The live product has two different library counts that should be explained rather than conflated: the public catalog displays **154 top-level entries**, while production storage contains **273 ready records**, including **119 child texts** inside larger collections. Similarly, the product has **29 published courses**, but only **PRE, C01, and FD01** are currently release-open; PRE is the free orientation path and the other two are paid paths.

### Verified scale

| Asset | Current production scale | Pricing meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| Published curriculum | 29 courses; 229 course weeks; 592 reading assignments | A substantial curriculum asset, but not the same as 29 courses available today |
| Release-open curriculum | 3 courses: PRE, C01, FD01 | This is the course value a customer can actually access now |
| Original reading support | 589 live long-form reading digests | Strong editorial value and a meaningful substitute for assembling a reading plan alone |
| Library | 154 top-level catalog entries; 273 ready records including child texts | A durable primary-source foundation; metadata and licensing still need cleanup |
| Retrieval corpus | 5,158 embedded chunks | Grounds research output in the platform's library rather than generic generation alone |
| Knowledge graph | 1,980 entities, 32,256 relationships, 9,670 sourced claims | A major proprietary research asset and one of the strongest product differentiators |
| Course work | 194 micro-artifacts, 194 synthesis prompts, 187 tool exercises, 175 lens exercises, 164 completion pathways | Evidence that courses are structured learning experiences rather than reading lists |
| Core public tools | Library, Seven Lenses, Concept Search, Knowledge Graph, Study Journal | A clear five-tool product story |

The public site correctly describes Prismarium as a shared study space rather than an expert delivering a single final answer. That positioning should remain. The paid promise is not “more content.” It is **a structured way to develop an informed interpretation of one's own**.

## Feature value model

The score below measures willingness-to-pay potential, not whether a feature should be paywalled.

- **5 — Tier anchor:** can independently motivate purchase or is unusually differentiated.
- **4 — Strong upgrade driver:** materially improves the promised outcome.
- **3 — Retention driver:** makes the product useful repeatedly and harder to replace.
- **2 — Supporting value:** improves trust, convenience, or discovery.
- **1 — Expected foundation:** necessary product hygiene, not a reason to pay more.

“Maturity” is equally important. A valuable prototype should not appear on a price card as finished value.

### Monetizable value hierarchy

From strongest purchase driver to weakest, the current product value is:

1. Structured courses joined to reading digests, workbooks, artifacts and capstones.
2. Seven Lenses / Stelloquy as a distinctive, source-aware research method.
3. The curated library and full reader joined to annotations, collections and course reading.
4. The large sourced Knowledge Graph.
5. Deep Search and research history after gating and metering are repaired.
6. The interconnected Study Journal as a durable record of the learner's thinking.
7. The Working after its response defect and cost controls are repaired.
8. Peer synthesis, forums, chat, videos and Shared Workings after a real community forms.
9. Discovery, onboarding, profiles, voting and other supporting utilities.
10. Tarot, media, premium TTS, journal AI and advanced course graphs, which remain beta or incomplete and have no defensible current price contribution.

This ordering matters because the public price story should lead with the first six outcomes. A long list of small utilities makes the product look cheaper, not more valuable.

### Important non-features

The following should not support the present price until completed: formal certificates, verifiable credentials, graded exams, instructor or mentor review, live classes, office hours, coaching, accreditation, meaningful badges, finished premium neural TTS, a finished media library, dependable course-concept graphs, journal AI actions, and production-ready Tarot/ritual tools.

| Concept lookup | No | No | Yes | Yes | Yes |
| The Working | No | Yes, unmetered | Yes, unmetered | Yes, unmetered | Yes, unmetered |
| Community | Read varies | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |

### Trust-breaking mismatches to correct first

| Current promise or impression | Enforced reality | Required correction |
|---|---|---|
| Reader receives five AI queries each month | Reader receives one lifetime Seven Lenses trial with no reset | Choose one policy and use a single entitlement source in UI and APIs |
| Reader receives 25 journal pages | API allows 50 non-archived pages | Advertise 50 or deliberately change enforcement |
| Concept Search is Scholar+ | Concept lookup is available to any paid tier; Deep Search gating and language are inconsistent | Define “course search” versus “advanced research” clearly |
| Ongoing Seven Lenses requires Scholar/Adept | Student is treated as premium and receives five uses | Replace the blunt quota with included course-linked use plus one independent monthly inquiry; Scholar receives self-directed fair use |
| “Full course access” sounds like all 29 courses | Payment only unlocks courses that are also release-open | Say “all released paid courses,” display what is open now, and publish the release cadence |
| Deep Search is quota-limited | It checks but never records a search | Fix metering before treating quotas as tier value |
| Full graph access appears delivered | The data API returned records, but a production guest browser check displayed zero nodes | Verify authenticated and guest graph flows before using it as conversion proof |
| The Working is included free with an account | It incurs AI cost, has no quota, and likely has a response-shape bug | Repair, meter and decide its true free/paid boundary |
| Adept is the highest-value plan | It currently adds mainly 25 more AI queries and vague early access | Remove it from new sales and reserve the name until a distinct outcome exists |
| Courses imply eventual completion | Course-level progress is not reliably advanced and no credential exists | Build completion rules before marketing completion evidence |

These inconsistencies are pricing problems because willingness to pay depends on trust. Fixing them is worth more than a cleverer headline price.

## Recommended tiers, least to most value

### 1. The Reader — Explore

**Price:** Free forever  
**Promise:** “Read trustworthy sources, explore connections, and begin your own grimoire.”

Include:

- Every public YouTube video and its public Prismarium companion resources.
- Public catalog, curriculum map, course previews, blog and populated video resources.
- PRE orientation path.
- Full library after account creation, including the reader and browser read-aloud.
- Base Knowledge Graph and source dossiers once the rendering issue is fixed.
- Bookmarks, collections, annotations and basic reading progress.
- Up to 50 active journal pages, wiki-links and backlinks.
- Forum/community participation.
- One lifetime Seven Lenses demonstration.
- One lifetime preview of The Working after it is repaired and metered.

Why keep this generous: Reader is not a crippled trial; it is Prismarium's accessibility promise, trust engine and acquisition loop. Primary sources and a meaningful notebook let people benefit even when they cannot pay. Payment begins where the product supplies structured progression, repeated compute and deeper outcomes.

Do not market “five AI queries/month” unless that allowance is genuinely funded and enforced.

### 2. The Student — Learn

**Founding price:** $15/month  
**Launch-ready price:** $19/month or $190/year  
**Promise:** “Complete guided study paths and turn your reading into finished work.”

Include everything in Reader, plus:

- Every **release-open** paid course, with an honest count shown on the pricing page.
- Weekly questions, tiered reading routes and all available reading digests.
- Exercises, worked examples, micro-artifacts, synthesis prompts and capstones.
- Course workbooks and unlimited journal pages.
- Course progress and community synthesis.
- Unlimited access to non-generative course tools: Library reading, annotations, collections, Knowledge Graph browsing, journal and workbook activity.
- Every Seven Lenses or Deep Search run explicitly assigned by an enrolled course, without those required runs consuming the member's independent-use allowance.
- One additional **Open Inquiry** per billing month outside coursework, usable for either Seven Lenses or Deep Search. This provides a taste of independent use without pretending Student is a full research plan.
- One intentional regeneration for each course-linked generative exercise; technical failures never count and may be retried so they cannot block completion.
- A verifiable **Certificate of Completion** for legitimately completed courses once the completion system is built.

This is the accessible paid tier. At $19 it is less than $5 per week and must be sufficient to receive the complete learning outcome. A Student should never encounter “upgrade to continue” after clicking a tool exercise inside a course. Do not reserve required tools or basic proof of completion for wealthier users.

The currently release-open format-v2 courses clarify the workload. PRE contains two prescribed “choose one” product-practice slots. C01 contains seven optional weekly product-practice slots, and FD01 contains five—approximately one opportunity per study week before the capstone. These may use Concept Search, Seven Lenses or the Knowledge Graph; none currently prescribes The Working, and the ordinary `lens_exercise` field is a manual worksheet rather than an AI run. Required costly-AI usage can therefore be zero when a learner chooses Graph, but the intended experience still offers roughly four product-practice sessions per month. Course-linked entitlement is safer and fairer than guessing which tool each learner will choose or how quickly they will progress.

If course-linked use cannot be separated technically at first, give Student **five successful generative sessions per month** as the temporary fallback: approximately four weekly practice opportunities plus one independent taste. Replace that blunt allowance with assignment-scoped access as soon as possible.

### 3. The Scholar — Independent research and tools

**Founding price:** $29/month  
**Launch-ready price:** $39/month or $390/year  
**Promise:** “Use Prismarium as a standalone research workspace—no course required.”

Include everything in Student, plus:

- Generous independent use across Seven Lenses and Deep Search, unrelated to course enrollment. Publicly describe this as **full self-directed tool access under fair use** rather than foregrounding credits.
- Course-assigned runs remain included and do not count against independent-use fair-use safeguards.
- Full Deep Search with reliable metering and clearly cited library excerpts.
- Research history, saved lens presets/defaults, continuity across investigations and better export.
- Advanced graph research views, course overlays or collections **only after they exist**; never gate source provenance itself.
- A separate bounded allowance for The Working after it is repaired; begin conservatively and set the public number only after cost telemetry exists.
- Priority support with a concrete response target if operationally supportable.

The Scholar pricing card should list the tool suite first: Seven Lenses, Deep Search, Knowledge Graph, Library, connected Journal and The Working. “All released courses included” may appear near the bottom as an optional extra, not as the headline value.

Scholar should be visually recommended because it best represents Prismarium's differentiated product. Its price is supported by the combination of curriculum, research software, corpus and notebook—not by “25 AI prompts.” Quotas are fair-use and cost controls, not the value story.

Crucially, the $39 target is defensible even for a member who never opens a course. Their outcome is faster, deeper, better-organized independent investigation across a curated corpus and multiple interpretive frameworks.

Do not advertise “unlimited AI” yet. Seven Lenses invokes multiple model calls and The Working also incurs generation cost. Say **“full research access with generous fair use.”** Internally begin with a conservative soft threshold, record the real cost of every successful run, and raise it until normal individual research almost never encounters the boundary. Failed runs should never count. Prohibit automation, bulk extraction, account sharing and resale rather than punishing ordinary deep use. All non-generative research—Library, Graph, ordinary search, journal, annotations, collections and saved material—can remain unlimited.

### 4. The Adept — Reserved, not currently for sale

**Current recommendation:** Remove the public checkout option and do not assign a launch price.  
**Internal status:** Reserved future tier.

Prismarium is not ready for Adept. The current package is mostly Scholar with a larger query allowance and early-access language. Selling it now adds complexity without a clear additional outcome.

Keep the name and internal entitlement available for future migration, but relaunch it only when several of these are real:

- A mature applied-practice workspace.
- A public/private portfolio dossier containing selected artifacts, capstones and completion records.
- A sustainable rubric-based capstone or practice review.
- A verified “Reviewed” distinction.
- Mature practitioner tools with measured costs and adoption.
- A sustainable live or feedback benefit.

If anyone already pays for Adept, grandfather them and communicate individually before changing access. For new customers, hide the card or use a no-price “Adept is being formed” waitlist. Do not use customers to fund an undefined promise.

### Suggested pricing-card language

Lead with outcomes, then show a short proof list:

| Tier | Outcome headline | Proof, not feature clutter |
|---|---|---|
| Reader | Explore sources and begin your notebook | Library, graph, free orientation, 50 journal pages |
| Student | Complete guided paths without tool paywalls | Released courses, course-required tool runs, workbooks, digests and capstones |
| Scholar | Research independently—no course required | Generous Seven Lenses and Deep Search use, graph, library and connected journal |

Adept should not appear as a purchasable card yet.

## Why these prices are defensible

No desk analysis can discover a single objectively correct price without conversion, retention, willingness-to-pay and cost data. These are evidence-backed launch hypotheses.

### Value ceiling: the replacement bundle

A serious Prismarium member might otherwise assemble several separate products: a guided learning membership, a read-it-later/highlighting system, an AI research tool, source texts, a note system and an esoteric study community. Current official prices illustrate that fragmented alternative:

- [Readwise Reader](https://readwise.io/pricing/reader) is $9.99/month when billed annually or $12.99 monthly.
- [Elicit](https://elicit.com/pricing) lists $132/year and $480/year research plans.
- [Coursera Plus](https://www.coursera.org/courseraplus/) lists $59/month or $399/year and includes completion certificates for eligible programs.
- [Peterson Academy](https://petersonacademy.com/) lists $399/year.
- [The School of Life](https://www.theschooloflife.com/membership/) lists £49.99/year for content and £79.99/year for community membership.

Prismarium does not duplicate these products, but the bundle establishes room for a $39 research-centered membership when its integrated outcome is communicated and delivered reliably.

### Market bands

| Market band | Current examples | What buyers are actually paying for |
|---|---|---|
| Free to roughly $200/year | [Quareia](https://www.quareia.com/how-to) is free; [Witch School](https://www.witchschool.com/subscriptions) lists free, $20/month and $90/year; [Witch Academy](https://www.witchacademy.org/enroll/) lists about $102.89 for 13 months or $180 lifetime | Access, self-direction, community and broad content; large content volume alone does not force a high price |

Prismarium's active $190/$390 annual ladder sits sensibly across these bands: accessible structured study and a serious research membership. Reserving Adept avoids imitating premium status pricing that the product cannot yet substantiate.

## Certificate analysis

### The certificate itself has almost no defensible standalone premium

The market demonstrates that an e-certificate is a commodity. [Magick & Witchcraft Academy](https://courses.magickandwitchcraft.com/courses/witchcraft-foundation-course/) advertises a completion e-certificate in a course listed at $24 sale/$84 normal. [Centre of Excellence](https://www.centreofexcellence.com/shop/ancient-egyptian-magic-diploma-course/) lists individual courses around £127 and an annual membership around £192, with an optional separate certificate fee. At the other end, the Boulder example pairs its $997 price with live supervised practice and recognized continuing-education hours, while explicitly noting that training is not a professional license.

The conclusion is straightforward:

> A certificate is valuable when it verifies a meaningful process. A decorative PDF does not make the process meaningful.

### What Prismarium should build

Prismarium already has better raw material for an honest completion record than many certificate-first products: required readings, structured exercises, synthesis work and capstones.

A basic completion credential should include:

- Learner display name.
- Course title and stable course version.
- Stated learning outcomes and estimated learning hours.
- Required activities and completion criteria.
- Issue date and unique credential ID.
- Public verification URL with opt-out privacy controls.
- Link to an optional learner-selected artifact or capstone.
- Clear issuer language: “Certificate of Completion issued by Prismarium.”

It should **not** say accredited, licensed, formally qualified, or certified practitioner unless an appropriate external body and real assessment process support that claim.

Tier policy:

- Student and Scholar should receive the same honest automated Certificate of Completion after meeting the requirements.
- A future Adept plan may receive a separate “Reviewed Capstone” distinction only after a human applies a published rubric and provides feedback.
- Never charge hundreds of dollars merely to unlock the PDF.

This protects accessibility while preserving a genuinely scarce outcome that could justify Adept later.

## Accessibility without chronic undervaluation

Accessibility and confident pricing are compatible. The ethical structure is a generous free foundation plus targeted access mechanisms—not artificially low pricing for every customer regardless of ability to pay.

Recommended safeguards:

1. **Keep the public channel and Reader useful.** Maintain a substantial body of complete public videos plus the library, base graph, free orientation, personal annotations and a bounded journal.
2. **Include two months free annually.** The $190/$390 prices make the discount clear.
3. **Create Community Access.** Offer a simple self-attested 40–50% discount for six or twelve months, renewable without invasive documentation and subject to a sustainable seat budget.
4. **Use regional purchasing-power discounts.** A 30–50% adjustment in lower-income regions is better than one globally depressed list price.
5. **Grandfather founders.** Reward people who supported the product during its rougher stage.
6. **Offer gifts and sponsored seats.** Let members fund another learner directly.
7. **Consider a one-course pass later.** A $79–$149 non-subscription path can serve people who want one course and cannot maintain a membership. Build annual billing first.
8. **Avoid lifetime plans.** Ongoing AI, storage, support and curriculum costs make lifetime access structurally risky.

The public message can be direct: “Reader will remain free. Paid memberships fund new courses, source curation, research tools and community-access seats.”

## Launch gates before raising prices

### Gate 1 — Make every promise true

- Establish one canonical entitlement definition shared by pricing UI, checkout, gates and API enforcement.
- Align Reader's journal and AI limits.
- Fix Deep Search accounting and choose whether it shares or has a separate allowance.
- Repair and meter The Working and AI Tarot before broader release.
- Test the Knowledge Graph as guest, Reader and paid member.
- Replace “all courses” with “all released paid courses” everywhere.

### Gate 2 — Deliver enough paid learning value

- Have at least **three paid courses** release-open, not counting PRE.
- Publish a believable course-release cadence.
- Finish course-level progress advancement and completion requirements.
- Upgrade or clearly label legacy-format courses before release.
- Clean obvious library metadata and record rights/license status for customer-facing texts.

### Gate 3 — Separate course use from independent use

- Pass verified course, week and exercise context when a learner opens Seven Lenses or Search from an assignment. Current course links only prefill a prompt and do not prove course context.
- Grant one successful assignment-scoped run plus one intentional regeneration without consuming the Student's monthly Open Inquiry; technical failures never count.
- Do not count failed generations.
- Keep Graph browsing and ordinary low-cost search unmetered.
- If this system is not ready, use the temporary five-runs-per-month Student allowance so normal weekly practice plus one independent taste remains possible.
- Meter Scholar's self-directed generative use under a transparent fair-use policy and record cost per feature before choosing a hard public ceiling.

### Gate 4 — Make billing safe

- Add annual Stripe prices and test checkout, upgrades, downgrades, cancellation and customer portal behavior.
- Decide and document grandfathering before changing public prices.
- Add AI cost telemetry by feature, plan and member.

## Validation plan

Pricing should be updated from real buyer evidence, not confidence alone.

### Before the public increase

Interview 15–30 people across four groups: active free learners, people who completed at least one week, paying/founding users, and qualified prospects who did not buy.

Ask outcome questions before price questions:

- What were you trying to understand or complete?
- What did you use before Prismarium?
- Which part would be hardest to replace?
- What would make you cancel after one month?
- Would a completion record matter, and to whom would you show it?
- Would human feedback change the value? What kind?

Then use the Van Westendorp questions for Student and Scholar: at what price does each feel too cheap to trust, a bargain, expensive but possible, and too expensive? Use a short MaxDiff exercise to rank courses, included course-tool use, independent Seven Lenses/Deep Search, library, graph, journal, The Working, community and certificate. Test Adept only after its future outcome is concrete.

### Metrics after launch

Track by plan and acquisition source:

- Reader-to-paid conversion.
- Enrollment and first-week activation.
- Week-two and week-four progression.
- Course completion and artifact creation.
- Seven Lenses, Deep Search and Working usage and direct cost.
- Monthly-to-annual conversion.
- Student/Scholar plan mix and the share choosing guided-learning versus independent-research onboarding.
- Month-one and month-two retention, cancellation reasons and refunds.
- Support minutes and review time per member.
- Community Access uptake and renewal.

Do not optimize for initial checkout conversion alone. A confident price is successful only when members activate, remain, complete meaningful work and recommend the product.

## Final recommendation

1. Use three active plans: Reader, Student and Scholar. Remove Adept from new checkout and grandfather any existing Adept members through individual communication.
2. Position the active tiers around outcomes: **Explore → Learn → Research**.
3. Preserve **$0 / $15 / $29** temporarily as Founding pricing, then move new customers to **$0 / $19 / $39**, with **$190 / $390 annual billing**, after the launch gates are met.
4. Make Scholar the recommended plan.
5. Keep Reader genuinely useful and fund accessibility with Community Access, regional pricing, sponsored seats and grandfathering.
6. Include a trustworthy basic completion certificate for every paid learner once completion works.
7. Include every course-assigned tool run with Student, plus one independent Open Inquiry each month; use five total monthly runs only as the temporary implementation fallback.
8. Give Scholar full self-directed tool access under generous fair use while keeping low-cost research tools unlimited.
9. Maintain substantial free public video and use Prismarium as the direct membership workspace where viewers continue, organize and apply what they discover; keep member-only creator extras optional and low-pressure.
10. Present Student and Scholar as two entry intentions—guided learning and independent research—so tool-only members never feel forced into a course product.
11. Reserve Adept until an applied-practice or reviewed-work outcome is real enough to deserve its own price.

Prismarium deserves to be priced with self-respect. The strongest expression of that is not copying a $997 certificate claim. It is clearly naming the unusually rich outcome already being built, making every promise true, and charging more to customers who want deeper structure, research and verified practice while leaving a real door open for everyone else.
