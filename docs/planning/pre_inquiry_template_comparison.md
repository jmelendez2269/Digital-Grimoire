## Living Question

When incompatible accounts of reality each seem to illuminate something, must we decide which one is true—or can learning to hold them change how we inquire?

Underneath that question is another:

> Is meaning discovered, created, or something that emerges between us and the world?

## Why I Am Here

I did not arrive here because I had already read these books or settled what they mean. I arrived because I keep encountering different versions of truth that feel impossible to dismiss and impossible to combine cleanly.

Part of me is drawn to the perennialist possibility that different traditions sometimes touch related aspects of reality. Another part of me takes seriously the existential possibility that nothing is inherently connected and that human beings create the meaning we experience. I have had to learn to hold possibilities like these instead of forcing myself to resolve them. That has mattered to me personally, including for my own sense of safety.

This course is not me teaching the answer. It is an invitation to read these works together, notice what each one actually says, and document what happens to our questions.

## Invitation

Human beings have long named truths, built systems around them, challenged them, and lived by them. We still disagree about what truth is and whether scientific, historical, moral, mathematical, religious, and personal truths are even the same kind of thing.

Perhaps this disagreement is not merely a failure humanity has not solved. Perhaps the impulse to seek, name, defend, doubt, and remake meaning is part of what makes us human. PRE begins there.

## Starting Position

Before reading, you may record one or two sentences:

- What is something you believe may be true even though you cannot fully prove it?
- What competing possibility do you not want to erase?

This is a timestamp, not a commitment. You may skip it.

## Tensions We Will Keep Open

- contradiction as error / contradiction as signal;
- truth discovered / meaning created / meaning emerging;
- empirical knowledge / other ways of knowing;
- interpretation / projection;
- disciplined practice / unplanned insight;
- connection / coincidence.

## What This Inquiry May Help You Practice

- Notice the urge to resolve an uncomfortable contradiction.
- Distinguish logical contradiction from different kinds or levels of claim.
- Compare how several texts respond to uncertainty without treating them as interchangeable.
- Use Prismarium's tools to follow a question rather than confirm a conclusion.
- Record how your own position changes, fractures, or becomes more precise.

## Scope, Limits, and Care

This inquiry does not teach Taoism, Zen Buddhism, Buddhism, Hindu traditions, or Scottish Enlightenment philosophy as wholes. It does not authorize religious practice or claim that these sources secretly teach the same doctrine.

The current source set relies heavily on older public-domain translations. Those translations make access possible but can carry dated language, colonial framing, or disputed interpretive choices. Production revision should add edition metadata, modern scholarly context, and tradition-aware review.

If holding incompatible possibilities becomes destabilizing rather than generative, stop. Ground yourself in what is immediate and verifiable, speak with someone you trust, or leave the question alone. No exercise is required.

# Week 1 — What Happens When Our Truths Collide?

## This Week's Living Question

When two claims seem meaningful but cannot both be right in the same way, are we encountering an error, a limit in our categories, or an invitation to look differently?

## Why This Question Now

Before comparing systems of truth, we need to notice our own response to contradiction. Do we investigate, defend, merge, rank, dismiss, or freeze?

## Source Constellation

### Suggested First Encounter: *Tao Te Ching*, Chapter 1

Use the existing digest and Keystone/Passage/Full Text tiers. Begin here because the chapter immediately complicates the relationship between reality and the names we give it.

**Context note:** Do not treat one translation as transparent access to an original, singular meaning. Compare wording when possible.

### *The Gateless Gate*, Case 1

Use the existing digest and three reading tiers. Encounter it as a historically situated Zen text, not as generic evidence that logic is inadequate.

**Context note:** The claim that “Mu means neither yes nor no” requires careful review; translations and interpretations of the case are more complicated than that formula suggests.

### Hume, *An Enquiry Concerning Human Understanding*, Section IV

Use the existing digest and three reading tiers. Hume introduces a different problem: how expectations built from experience acquire justification.

**Context note:** Let Hume disagree. Do not convert skepticism about induction into proof that all truth claims are equally uncertain.

## Inquiry Paths — Choose 1–2, More If You Want

### Concept Search

Search one concept—**truth**, **certainty**, **naming**, **contradiction**, or **experience**. Follow 2–3 results and notice whether the word keeps the same meaning.

### Knowledge Graph

Choose one source node and follow one path to another source or concept. Record the relationship the graph proposes, then ask: is this a historical connection, a conceptual similarity, or an editorial association?

### Seven Lenses

Choose 1–2 questions:

- **Philosophical:** Are these texts describing contradictions, category errors, or limits of language?
- **Religious/Spiritual:** What changes when a text is read within a path of practice rather than as an abstract argument?
- **Psychological:** What urge appears in you when an answer remains unavailable?
- **Historical:** What is lost when texts from different eras are placed in one conversation?

### Source Comparison

Compare any two sources. Write one sentence about a real similarity and one about a difference that should not be collapsed.

### Journal

Name one contradiction you are living with. You may describe what holding it feels like without deciding what it means.

## Pause Point

Are Lao Tzu, Mumon, and Hume pointing toward the same human problem, or have we created the resemblance by placing them together?

Optional ways in—choose one or two:

- “The resemblance becomes strongest when…”
- “The comparison breaks down when…”
- “I notice that I want them to agree because…”
- “The source that most resists this course's premise is…”

## Optional Inquiry Trace — Contradiction Portrait

Preserve any combination of:

- two incompatible statements;
- a note about what holding them feels like;
- one source comparison;
- one graph path or concept trail;
- one question you cannot yet answer.

## What Remains Open

- Are all contradictions worth holding?
- When does “holding both” become avoidance?
- Can sources be compared without turning difference into sameness?

# Week 2 — Can Our Relationship to Uncertainty Change?

## This Week's Living Question

Can we become more capable of living with unresolved questions, and if so, is that capacity trained, discovered, received, or invented?

## Why This Question Now

Week 1 notices contradiction. Week 2 asks what happens over time without assuming that the desired outcome is permanent comfort with uncertainty.

## Source Constellation

### Suggested First Encounter: *Zhuangzi*, Butterfly Dream and Cook Ding

Use the existing digest and three reading tiers. Read the two stories together without forcing them into a single theory of “effortless mastery.”

### *The Dhammapada*, Chapters 1–2

Use the existing digest and reading tiers. Attend to the text's ethical and religious setting rather than extracting “mind training” as a neutral productivity method.

### *Kena Upanishad*

Use the existing digest and reading tiers. Let its account of knowing remain culturally and philosophically specific.

**Context note:** The current course language around “the Witness” and “unknowing-that-knows” should be checked against a responsible modern translation and scholarship before production revision.

## Inquiry Paths — Choose 1–2, More If You Want

### Concept Search

Search one concept—**practice**, **attention**, **effort**, **grace**, **knowing**, or **transformation**. Follow 2–3 results and mark where the concept changes traditions.

### Knowledge Graph

Trace one path among *Zhuangzi*, *The Dhammapada*, and *Kena Upanishad*. Ask whether the graph reveals a documented relationship or creates a useful modern juxtaposition.

### Seven Lenses

Choose 1–2 questions:

- **Philosophical:** What kind of self is assumed to be doing the training?
- **Religious/Spiritual:** What commitments disappear when practice is translated into a general psychological skill?
- **Psychological:** Can tolerance for uncertainty be practiced without romanticizing distress?
- **Scientific:** What could empirical research measure here, and what would remain outside that measurement?
- **Ethical:** When might encouraging someone to remain with contradiction cause harm?

### Source Comparison

Choose any two texts and compare what each thinks effort can accomplish. Preserve one disagreement between them.

### Journal

Recall one time when you stopped forcing an answer. Record what changed and what did not. You do not need to label the experience with one of the traditions.

## Pause Point

If our relationship to uncertainty changes, what changed: our beliefs, our attention, our nervous system, our language, our circumstances, or the reality we encountered?

Optional ways in—choose one or two:

- “The training explanation accounts for… but not…”
- “The grace or emergence explanation accounts for… but not…”
- “A scientific account might measure…”
- “I do not yet know whether…”

## Optional Inquiry Record — Holding Two Things

Choose 1–3 traces from either week:

- your starting position;
- a source you resisted;
- a comparison that held and one that failed;
- a graph path or concept trail;
- a lens question that changed your framing;
- a contradiction that remains alive;
- a new question you want to follow.

End, if useful, with three provisional statements:

1. **Right now, I suspect…**
2. **I can also imagine…**
3. **What remains open is…**

## Door Forward

What if the goal is neither to prove that everything connects nor to prove that nothing does, but to become more honest about how we form, test, and live inside those possibilities?
