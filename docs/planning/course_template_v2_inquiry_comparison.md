# Prismarium Course Template — Inquiry Model
## Comparison Draft v2 — Not Adopted

> **Status:** Editorial proposal only. This document does not replace the production template and is not wired into the app.
>
> **Purpose:** Test what a Prismarium course looks like when it is hosted as a shared investigation rather than delivered as settled teaching. The library remains abundant; participation remains humane.

---

## COURSE ORIENTATION

### Course Metadata

Retain all current production metadata and family-taxonomy fields.

### Living Question

The genuine question carrying the course. It must remain open enough that the course can complicate the founder's position as well as the participant's.

### Why I Am Here

A short first-person founder note that states:

- what drew Jack to the question;
- what he currently suspects or feels;
- what competing possibility he is unwilling to erase;
- what he has not read, mastered, or resolved.

This is intellectual location, not authority signaling.

### Invitation

Explain why someone else might want to investigate this question alongside us. Do not promise revelation, transformation, expertise, or a final answer.

### Starting Position

Invite the participant to leave a brief record of where they begin. This may be a sentence, voice note, sketch, or skipped entirely.

### Tensions We Will Keep Open

Name 3–6 live possibilities. They need not be binary oppositions when the inquiry contains a third or fourth possibility.

### What This Inquiry May Help You Practice

List 4–6 capacities using invitational verbs: notice, compare, distinguish, trace, test, question, revise, or hold.

### Scope, Limits, and Care

State:

- what the course investigates;
- what it does not teach or authorize;
- where the source collection is historically or culturally limited;
- which claims require modern scholarship or tradition-aware review;
- any emotional, spiritual, or practice-related safety note.

### Source Map

Show the full source constellation before Week 1:

- 2–4 sources per week;
- why each source is present;
- where sources disagree;
- translation, edition, and rights metadata;
- modern context or review still needed.

The number of sources is not the learner's workload. Digests and reading tiers make the library navigable.

## WEEKLY INQUIRY

### Week [N] — [Question-Led Title]

#### This Week's Living Question

One question that neither the host nor participant is expected to settle.

#### Why This Question Now

A short bridge from the previous week and a note about what could become more complicated.

#### Source Constellation

Preserve 2–4 selected works. For each:

- selection rationale;
- digest;
- Keystone;
- Passage;
- Full Text;
- context or limits note.

Label one **Suggested First Encounter**. This is a doorway, not the only important source.

#### Inquiry Paths — Choose What Pulls You

Offer the complete useful menu. Recommend choosing **1–2 paths**, doing more if desired, or doing none.

Possible paths include:

- **Concept Search:** one suggested concept trail;
- **Knowledge Graph:** one suggested route between nodes;
- **Seven Lenses:** a set of lens questions, with a recommendation to choose 1–2;
- **Source Comparison:** one tension to examine across texts;
- **Journal:** one invitation to record movement or uncertainty.

Each path must explain what the tool can help reveal. It must not imply that the tool proves a predetermined interpretation.

#### Pause Point

One provisional synthesis question with 2–4 optional entry prompts. Recommend choosing 1–2 prompts, not answering all of them.

#### Optional Inquiry Trace

A small artifact for participants who want to preserve where they are:

- a paragraph;
- a two-column comparison;
- one saved graph path;
- a concept trail;
- a voice note;
- a sketch;
- or another form that fits.

It records movement, not completion.

#### What Remains Open

End with unresolved questions, disagreements among sources, and directions the participant may carry forward.

---

## FINAL WEEK / INQUIRY RECORD

The final artifact is optional. It gathers selected traces without requiring a verdict.

Suggested components:

- where I began;
- one source that changed or complicated my language;
- one source I resisted;
- one graph path, concept trail, or lens response;
- what I currently suspect;
- what remains unresolved;
- where I want to go next.

Participants choose 1–3 traces or create something new. The final question is a door, not a test.

## DESIGN RULES

1. The host is visibly participating in the inquiry.
2. Questions remain capable of changing the course's own premise.
3. Preserve the rich source library and its digests.
4. Distinguish source abundance from assigned workload.
5. Keep 2–4 sources per week; identify a suggested doorway without demoting the rest.
6. Offer a rich activity menu; recommend choosing 1–2 paths.
7. Lens questions, graph routes, searches, and journal prompts are recommendations.
8. Do not imply that completion establishes mastery.
9. Describe traditions specifically and historically; avoid using them as interchangeable proof of a universal claim.
10. Distinguish primary sources, historical interpretation, modern scholarship, and the host's own response.
11. Name uncertainty, missing context, translation limits, and needed review.
12. Let participants hold, revise, reject, or leave unanswered what they encounter.
