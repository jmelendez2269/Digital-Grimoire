# The Working

The **Working** is where you stop reading about practice and start *doing* it. You bring an intention — something you want to enact, not just study — and Prismarium assembles a ritual for you from its curated correspondence graph, then helps you record the casting and follow what unfolds.

A *working* is practitioner language for an active operation: distinct from study, distinct from divination. This is the doing side of the app. The [Library](/wiki/library-features) is where you read; the Working is where you act, record, and find out.

> [!NOTE]
> The Working is intent-driven and **generated**, not browsed. If you'd rather pick from pre-written rituals, see the [Ritual Library](/wiki/ritual-machine). The two are complementary — a Working you like can be saved as your own ritual.

> [!NOTE] **What's available now**
> The generator is live: **type any intention in plain words and receive a complete, graph-grounded ritual.** The *experiment* parts of this page — saving a working, auto-stamped casting conditions, the follow-up log, and community sharing — are on the way and described below as the intended experience.

## The flow

### 1. State your intent

Type what you're working toward in your own words — *"call in protection for my household,"* *"release grief and prepare for a new chapter," "steady my finances."* You can also pick from the intentions the graph already recognizes (abundance, protection, healing, manifestation, and many more).

### 2. The graph assembles your palette

Prismarium searches its correspondence graph for everything aligned with your intention and returns a palette of components — drawn from your curated data, not invented:

- **Stones and crystals** that carry the energy
- **Herbs and plants** traditionally used for it
- **Colors** that align
- **Timing** — moon phase, day of the week, season, celebration
- **Deities, runes, tarot, and archetypes** connected to the work

Each component arrives with its own explanation — *why* it connects, the tradition it comes from, and how confident that association is. Nothing in your palette is a black box.

### 3. A ritual is synthesized

The assembled palette is composed into an actual step-by-step ritual that uses *those specific components*. Because it's built from the graph you curated, the citrine in your ritual is there for a reason you can trace — not because a generic model guessed at it.

## A working is an experiment

This is the heart of it. A grimoire was always a magical record — you don't cast and walk away. The Working treats each ritual as an experiment with a lifecycle:

### Before — your hypothesis

Write your intent in your own words before you begin. What are you actually hoping shifts? What would success look like? Stating it up front means you can't quietly rewrite it later.

### The cast — your conditions

When you mark a working as done, it's timestamped — and because Prismarium already knows the moon phase, the day's planetary ruler, and the season, those conditions are recorded automatically. These are your experimental controls, captured the moment you cast.

### After — your follow-up

A working doesn't end at the casting. Keep an ongoing, timestamped log of what unfolds:

- *Day 3 — nothing yet.*
- *Day 9 — the callback came.*
- *Day 21 — still holding.*

You watch the result develop over time instead of judging it in the moment.

## Sharing with the community

A completed working — intent, ritual, conditions, and outcome notes — can be published to the community. Over time, as practitioners run similar workings and log what happened, the app starts to accumulate real practitioner experience alongside the curated tradition. The graph says citrine aligns with prosperity; the workings show whether practitioners who used it reported results.

> [!NOTE]
> Sharing is always your choice. Your intent, your notes, and your follow-ups stay private until you decide to publish a working.

## Tips for success

- **Be specific in your intent.** "Protection for my household after a break-in" produces a more focused palette than "protection."
- **Write your hypothesis honestly.** This is your private record first; authenticity is what makes the follow-up worth reading.
- **Keep logging after the cast.** The single most valuable part of a working is the trail of follow-ups — that's the evidence.
- **Review your past workings.** Patterns show up across many entries that you'll never see in one.
